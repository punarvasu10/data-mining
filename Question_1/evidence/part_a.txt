PART (A) PARTITIONING EVIDENCE
==============================

Layout:
sales/store_id=<store_id>/business_date=<YYYY-MM-DD>/data.parquet

Example query:
Store = S03
Month = October 2024

Files that can be considered:
31

Bytes:
407116

All sales objects:
4389

All sales bytes:
44236871

A single-folder layout could require scanning all 4389 objects
(44,236,871 bytes). With the partitioned layout, the same query
only needs to read the 31 objects under store_id=S03/
business_date=2024-10-*/ — a total of 407,116 bytes.

This demonstrates partition pruning: DuckDB reads ~0.92% of the
data volume (407,116 / 44,236,871) and skips 4358 of 4389 objects
(≈ 99.3%) because the store_id and business_date columns are
encoded in the path.
